# Reply shapes

Skeletons, not scripts. Fill them with the specifics of the actual message —
a reply that reads as a template is worse than a slow reply.

---

## Straightforward answer

> Hi {name},
>
> {the answer, first sentence, no preamble}
>
> {one line of useful detail — a timeframe, a link, a next step}
>
> {sign-off}

---

## Something went wrong

> Hi {name},
>
> {name the specific thing that went wrong — not "your experience"}
>
> {what happens now, with a real date}
>
> {what they need to do, if anything — ideally nothing}
>
> {sign-off}

One apology. In the first line. Not repeated in the last.

---

## We can't do the thing they asked

> Hi {name},
>
> {the no, plainly, in the first sentence}
>
> {why — one sentence, no policy recitation}
>
> {what we CAN do instead}
>
> {sign-off}

Never bury the no under three paragraphs of warmth. People read the first
line and the last.

---

## Need more information

> Hi {name},
>
> Happy to sort this out — I just need {the single specific thing}.
>
> {why it's needed, if it isn't obvious}
>
> {sign-off}

Ask for one thing. A list of four questions gets one answered.

---

## Escalation stub

When it isn't covered, still draft the holding reply:

> Hi {name},
>
> Thanks — I want to get you an accurate answer on this rather than a quick
> one, so I'm checking with the team and will come back to you {timeframe}.
>
> {sign-off}

Then flag it for the human, per SKILL.md.
